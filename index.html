About

whoami

It started with 100 Days of GPU — a personal experiment to see if I could actually learn CUDA properly. Turns out, with daily practice and a lot of late nights, you can. That experiment became fast-gpu-kernels, where I benchmarked hand-written BF16 GEMM across A100, H100 and B200 — ending at 258.7 TFLOPS on A100, 728.8 TFLOPS on H100, and 1840 TFLOPS on B200 (125% of cuBLAS peak). I was running these on Modal from my laptop, watching the numbers tick up one optimization at a time.

Then I got curious about LLM serving and built nano-dynamo — a minimal reimplementation of NVIDIA Dynamo in ~1.5k lines of Python. Benchmarked head-to-head on a full disaggregated stack (4× A100 · 2 prefill + 2 decode · Qwen3-14B-FP8), TTFT lands within ~1.3× of NVIDIA Dynamo — throughput and end-to-end latency within 3–9%. Same obsession with understanding what's actually happening under the hood. I'm drawn to problems where you can trace every microsecond to a specific hardware decision.

To actually learn distributed training, I wrote it instead of reading about it — a full training loop in plain torch.distributed, with a Llama-1B on 2×A100 as the test. GradSync is the result: hand-written tensor parallelism, from how the weights are sharded to how the gradients come back together, every collective visible. Everyone should do this once — you learn more in a week of writing than a year of tutorials.
